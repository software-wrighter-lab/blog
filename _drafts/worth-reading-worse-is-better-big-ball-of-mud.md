## The Rise of Worse Is Better

Richard P. Gabriel, ["The Rise of Worse Is Better"](https://www.dreamsongs.com/WorseIsBetter.html), a section of his essay "Lisp: Good News, Bad News, How to Win Big" (1989; published 1991). The MIT/Stanford approach puts correctness and consistency first; the New Jersey approach puts simplicity of implementation first and ships early. The second spreads like a virus, and wins.

## Big Ball of Mud

Brian Foote and Joseph Yoder, ["Big Ball of Mud"](http://www.laputan.org/mud/) (PLoP '97; in *Pattern Languages of Program Design 4*, 1999). The most common software architecture is none at all: sprawling, unstructured systems that grow under deadlines and survive because they work well enough. The paper treats it as a pattern, with the forces that produce it.
